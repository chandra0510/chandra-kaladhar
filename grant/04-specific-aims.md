## Abstract

Fusion-positive rhabdomyosarcoma is driven by PAX3-FOXO1, which is still not a drug target, and it has a high risk of metastasis. We showed that the fusion raises NOS1 and nitric oxide, suppresses TGFβ signaling, and loosens tumor-cell grip on the extracellular matrix. In three-dimensional spheroids, follower cells behind the invasive front have higher PAX3-FOXO1, higher proliferation, and higher WEE1 activity, and WEE1 blockade slows growth and invasion. Adavosertib plus irinotecan did not meet its efficacy bar in relapsed rhabdomyosarcoma. We propose that unselected relapsed disease was the wrong setting, and that WEE1 dependence sits in the PAX3-FOXO1-high follower compartment. This project defines that compartment and tests when WEE1 inhibition stops collective invasion.

## Specific aims

**Aim 1. Define the PAX3-FOXO1–WEE1 follower compartment during collective invasion.**
In fusion-positive spheroids we will measure, in the same tumors, PAX3-FOXO1 level, proliferation, WEE1 activity, and matrix-fiber alignment at the invasive front and in the follower zone. PAX3-FOXO1 knockdown, which our RNA-seq shows is negatively enriched for G1/S and DNA-replication programs, will test whether the fusion is required for this compartment. The aim resolves an open point in our preliminary data: higher proliferation in PAX3-FOXO1-high followers, alongside evidence that the fusion also alters S-phase and lowers pCDK2 and γH2AX.

**Aim 2. Test whether WEE1 inhibition blocks collective invasion in a PAX3-FOXO1-dependent manner.**
We will treat fusion-positive spheroids with adavosertib, the clinical WEE1 inhibitor, and use WEE1 knockdown as a specificity control. Endpoints are spheroid growth, radial invasion, and loss of the follower compartment. We will then ask whether the effect shrinks when PAX3-FOXO1 is reduced or when matrix anchorage is shifted through the NOS1–TGFβ pathway already published. A negative result will be reported and will argue against further unselected WEE1 trials in this disease.
