## Abstract

Fusion-positive rhabdomyosarcoma is driven by PAX3-FOXO1 and has a high risk of metastasis. The fusion protein itself is not a drug. Preliminary studies indicate that PAX3-FOXO1 maintains the heparan sulfate proteoglycan SPOCK3. Heparan sulfate proteoglycans capture growth factors such as FGF2 and present them to receptors that activate ERK and proliferation. They may also set cortical tension, which affects whether a cell can pass through a narrow space during extravasation. Knockdown of PAX3-FOXO1 lowers SPOCK3, weakens FGF2 capture and FGF2-driven ERK activation, slows proliferation, softens the cortex, and reduces passage through narrow pores. This project tests whether SPOCK3 is required for those effects, and whether the heparan sulfate antagonist surfen can reproduce them.

## Specific aims

**Aim 1. Determine whether SPOCK3 promotes proliferation by supporting FGF2–ERK signaling.**
We will knock out SPOCK3 with CRISPR-Cas9 in fusion-positive cells. Expansion microscopy will measure heparan sulfate abundance and FGF2 capture at nanoscale resolution. Proliferation will be measured by CCK-8 assay and live-cell imaging. ERK activation will be measured by biochemical assay. Surfen will be tested on the same endpoints as a pharmacologic block of heparan sulfate.

**Aim 2. Determine whether SPOCK3 supports extravasation by maintaining cortical tension.**
Atomic force microscopy will measure cortical tension after SPOCK3 knockout and after surfen. Transwell assays with narrow pores will test whether those same conditions reduce the cell’s ability to pass through a tight opening, the in vitro counterpart of extravasation. Together, the aims ask whether SPOCK3 is a functional downstream target of PAX3-FOXO1 in growth and spread.
