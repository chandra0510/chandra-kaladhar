## A. Personal statement

NIH Biographical Sketch Supplement: ≤3,500 characters. Parenthetical product refs only (no full citations or hyperlinks). Match products listed in the Common Form Products section. Confirm the “Kaladhar VC” sentence before upload if that name appears on older papers.

I am well suited for this project because of my training and recent experimental work at the interface of PAX3-FOXO1 biology, extracellular matrix (ECM), and cell-cycle control in fusion-positive rhabdomyosarcoma (FPRMS). As a postdoctoral fellow in the Park laboratory at Children’s Hospital Los Angeles / USC Keck School of Medicine, I work in a pediatric sarcoma environment with access to FPRMS/FNRMS models, confocal reflectance and immunofluorescence imaging, and mentor oversight from JinSeok Park, PhD.

Over the past three years I contributed to studies showing that PAX3-FOXO1 increases NOS1/nitric oxide, suppresses TGFβ signaling, and reduces cell–ECM interaction in FPRMS (Chronopoulos et al., 2025), and that ECM topography can drive phenotype switching in neuroblastoma (Chronopoulos, Vemula et al., 2025). I am first author on AACR abstracts reporting RMS matrix findings (Vemula et al., 2024) and PAX3-FOXO1–linked WEE1 dependence and cell-cycle phenotypes in FPRMS (Vemula and Park, 2026). These projects built the technical expertise required here: fusion knockdown/knockout, cell-cycle and replication-stress readouts, ECM-facing culture systems, and quantitative imaging.

This Rhabdo Coalition proposal tests whether WEE1 dependence persists—and where it persists—in tissue-like native muscle ECM. Aim 1 will establish and benchmark optically accessible decellularized skeletal muscle (dECM) scaffolds against patient/PDX histology and 2D/collagen/Matrigel cultures, including spatial PAX3-FOXO1 heterogeneity at ECM contact. Aim 2 will map cell-cycle states across alveolar-like niches and test WEE1 inhibition (including Debio-0123) to ask whether residual cells occupy defined architectural compartments. My role is to lead experimental design, dECM production/QC, reseeding and imaging assays, WEE1 genetic/pharmacologic studies, analysis, and sharing of methods with the RMS community under Dr. Park’s mentorship. I have also published under the name Kaladhar VC.

## C. Contributions to science

### 1. PAX3-FOXO1 reprograms cell–ECM interaction in rhabdomyosarcoma (~1,240 characters)

Historical background: Rhabdomyosarcoma (RMS) is the most common pediatric soft-tissue sarcoma and is divided into fusion-positive RMS (FPRMS), driven by PAX3-FOXO1, and fusion-negative RMS (FNRMS). FPRMS has poorer outcomes, yet how the fusion reshapes tumor-cell interaction with the extracellular matrix (ECM) was incompletely defined.

Central findings: We found that FNRMS cells engage the ECM more strongly through TGFβ signaling, whereas PAX3-FOXO1 increases NOS1/nitric oxide, suppresses TGFβ signaling, and reduces cell–ECM interaction in FPRMS (Chronopoulos, Chavez et al., 2025; Vemula et al., 2024). Removing the fusion restores matrix dependence and sensitizes cells to disruption of that interaction.

Influence: These findings identify matrix engagement and TGFβ signaling as fusion-controlled phenotypes relevant to metastatic behavior and suggest matrix-facing vulnerabilities that differ by RMS subtype, informing strategies to improve therapy for children with RMS.

My role: As a postdoctoral fellow at Children’s Hospital Los Angeles, I contributed to fusion-knockdown experiments, quantitative matrix imaging, and related functional assays in FPRMS models, including work presented at AACR 2024 (Vemula et al., 2024).

### 2. Extracellular-matrix topography drives neuroblastoma phenotype switching (~990 characters)

Historical background: Neuroblastoma phenotypes are strongly influenced by the tumor microenvironment, but how physical ECM architecture itself drives malignant state transitions was not fully clear.

Central findings: We showed that fibrous ECM topography can drive adrenergic-to-mesenchymal transition in neuroblastoma (Chronopoulos, Vemula et al., 2025). Matrix geometry therefore acts as an instructive cue for phenotype switching, beyond soluble signals alone.

Influence: This work advances pediatric solid-tumor mechanobiology by linking native-like matrix architecture to clinically relevant phenotype states, supporting the broader use of engineered and native ECM systems to study childhood cancers and therapy response.

My role: At Children’s Hospital Los Angeles, I contributed to matrix-facing experimental work and imaging analyses in this study (Chronopoulos, Vemula et al., 2025), building expertise in ECM systems that I continue to apply in pediatric sarcoma research.

### 3. PAX3-FOXO1, WEE1, and cell-cycle control in FPRMS (~1,200 characters)

Historical background: Fusion-positive rhabdomyosarcoma (FPRMS) depends on PAX3-FOXO1–driven proliferation, but how the fusion controls cell-cycle checkpoints—and whether WEE1 dependence persists in tissue-like architecture—remained incompletely defined. WEE1 is a G2/M checkpoint kinase and a candidate vulnerability downstream of the fusion.

Central findings: PAX3-FOXO1 knockdown reduces FPRMS growth, alters G1/S and DNA-replication programs, and induces S-phase arrest with lower CDK phosphorylation; chromatin reanalysis places WEE1 downstream of PAX3-FOXO1, and WEE1 knockout phenocopies these cell-cycle defects (Vemula and Park, 2026). Related work also links spatial ECM contact to reduced peripheral PAX3-FOXO1 in FPRMS tumors.

Influence: These findings nominate WEE1 as a functionally relevant effector of PAX3-FOXO1 and motivate testing whether, and where, WEE1 dependence is preserved in optically accessible native-muscle dECM models before clinical prioritization.

My role: I am first author on this work. I designed and performed the cell-cycle, knockdown/knockout, and WEE1-inhibition experiments and analyzed the associated transcriptomic enrichment results reported in the AACR 2026 abstract (Vemula and Park, 2026). A full manuscript from this project is under development.
