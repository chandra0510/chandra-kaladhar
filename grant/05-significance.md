# Significance and impact

Limit: one page. Paste everything under this line.

Children with fusion-positive rhabdomyosarcoma still relapse too often, and relapse after metastatic disease is rarely curable. PAX3-FOXO1 defines this subtype, but the fusion protein is not a medicine. Treatment remains chemotherapy, surgery, and radiation, largely shared with fusion-negative disease despite different biology. What is needed is a target downstream of the fusion, in the cell behavior that spreads the tumor, and a reason to think a drug against that target will work in the patients who resemble the laboratory model.

WEE1 looked like that target and then stalled. Chemical and genomic screens nominated it in rhabdomyosarcoma, and preclinical xenografts showed activity of adavosertib, including with irinotecan and vincristine. ADVL1312 then tested adavosertib plus irinotecan in relapsed disease. Ten patients with rhabdomyosarcoma enrolled. There were no objective responses. Two had stable disease for eight and ten cycles. Half the tumors were alveolar, and the cohort was heavily pretreated, often with prior irinotecan and with bulky or metastatic disease. That result says the combination was not enough in unselected relapse. It does not say whether WEE1 matters in one cell state inside fusion-positive tumors, or whether invasion, rather than shrinkage of a bulky mass, is the behavior it controls.

Our work is that missing state. PAX3-FOXO1 induces NOS1, raises nitric oxide, and suppresses TGFβ signaling and cell–matrix adhesion. Fusion-negative cells keep those adhesions and die when the adhesions or the TGFβ signal are disrupted. Removing the fusion restores matrix engagement. Looser anchorage is one explanation for metastatic fitness: the cells depend less on staying attached. In three-dimensional spheroids, a second pattern appears. Cells just behind the invasive front have higher PAX3-FOXO1, higher proliferation, and higher WEE1 activity. Knockdown of the fusion depletes G1/S and DNA-replication gene sets, and WEE1 inhibition slows growth and invasion. Fusion level is also uneven from tumor to tumor and, in our imaging, from region to region. A drug that matters only in the high-fusion follower zone would be easy to miss in an unselected trial scored by shrinkage.

Aim 1 asks whether PAX3-FOXO1 is required for the WEE1-high follower compartment and how that compartment sits relative to matrix engagement. Aim 2 asks whether WEE1 inhibition, with adavosertib and with knockdown, stops collective invasion only while that compartment is present. The NOS1–TGFβ pathway is the handle we already trust for changing anchorage, so we can tell whether WEE1 dependence and matrix independence are one state or two.

If the hypothesis holds, WEE1 is worth revisiting only in fusion-positive models that still have a proliferative follower compartment, not as another unselected relapse schedule. If it fails, the field gets a clear negative on the same clinical compound and can retire this use of WEE1. Either result is useful to the laboratories and family foundations choosing which ideas to carry. A biomarker and a justified combination are later steps. This award does not claim to finish them.
