## Rhabdomyosarcoma relevance

Limit: 150 words.

This project studies only fusion-positive rhabdomyosarcoma, the subtype driven by PAX3-FOXO1 and the group of children most likely to relapse with metastatic disease. The question is whether that fusion promotes growth and spread through one heparan sulfate proteoglycan, SPOCK3. SPOCK3 is the molecular target. The mechanism under test is capture of the growth factor FGF2, activation of ERK, and cortical stiffness that helps cells pass through narrow spaces during extravasation. The models are fusion-positive cells already in use in this laboratory, including cells in which PAX3-FOXO1 has been knocked down. Fusion-negative cells are a comparison. The tool compound is surfen, an antagonist of heparan sulfate. The result this award is built to deliver is whether blocking SPOCK3, or heparan sulfate more broadly, is a credible way to slow growth and metastatic spread in this cancer.

## Data, resource sharing, and collaboration

Limit: 200 words. (199 words below; revised for Ellen Cheney comments.)

We are committed to sharing this work with the rhabdomyosarcoma community nationally and internationally, while protecting CHLA intellectual property. Before releasing protocols or materials that could have commercialization value, we will confirm the plan with CHLA Technology Commercialization. After clearance, methods for skeletal muscle decellularization, scaffold QC, FPRMS reseeding, and in situ cell-cycle / WEE1-response imaging will be posted upon publication or within 12 months of the award end, whichever comes first. Validated dECM methods and, where material remains, scaffold aliquots or reproduction guidance will be shared with academic RMS laboratories under CHLA material-transfer agreements. Imaging datasets, measurements, analysis notes, and any new molecular data will be deposited in public repositories on the same timeline. We welcome collaboration with RMS investigators and consortia to adopt the dECM model, compare architectural readouts, and test agents beyond WEE1—to learn whether tissue-like architecture preserves or restricts therapeutic vulnerabilities and to speed better treatments for children with FPRMS. Results will also be presented at scientific conferences and professional meetings. During the one-year award we will give the Rhabdo Coalition a short nontechnical family update, plus a final report at award’s end. This project uses established cell lines and generates no identifiable patient-level data.

## Funding leverage and impact

Limit: 150 words. (140 words below.)

Rhabdo Coalition funding will change the direction of this work from monolayer culture to a native-muscle ECM model that better mirrors fusion-positive tumors in children. With this support, we will complete Aim 1 by establishing and benchmarking decellularized skeletal muscle scaffolds for fusion-positive RMS, and Aim 2 by mapping where WEE1 dependence is preserved across alveolar-like architecture. Without it, the project remains limited to plastic dishes and cannot generate the tissue-like evidence families and clinicians need before WEE1 drugs are prioritized for children. Coalition support expands the science beyond ongoing cell-line studies, creates a shareable dECM resource for the RMS community, and advances a clearer path toward architecture-aware therapy. As an early-career team building an independent RMS program, this award also helps launch the next generation of investigators working at the interface of fusion biology, tissue architecture, and targeted treatment.

## Partial funding

Limit: 150 words. (129 words below.)

Yes. At a minimum of $50,000 for one year, we would complete Aim 1 in full: produce QC-passed skeletal muscle dECM scaffolds, reseed FPRMS lines (Rh30/Rh41), benchmark alveolar-like architecture against histology and 2D/collagen/Matrigel by CRM and immunofluorescence, and establish FNRMS subtype comparisons. Aim 2 would be narrowed to core in situ mapping of cell-cycle and replication-stress markers (EdU, pHH3, pCDK, γH2A.X) on dECM, with a focused WEE1-inhibition arm in FPRMS only, without the full FNRMS drug comparison or extended dose/time matrix in the complete proposal. At this level we would still deliver a validated, shareable dECM FPRMS model and an initial spatial read on whether WEE1 dependence persists in tissue-like ECM. The timeline would remain 12 months; niche-level survival analyses and broader subtype pharmacology would wait for additional support.
