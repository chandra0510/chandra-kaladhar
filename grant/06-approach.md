## Strategy

Both aims use hanging-drop spheroids embedded in collagen I and imaged by confocal reflection microscopy, the assay this laboratory already uses to tell pulled matrix fibers from fibers that are only displaced. Rh41, including the PAX3-FOXO1-knockdown system already characterized, is the primary model. One additional authenticated fusion-positive line already in the laboratory is the replication model. A fusion-negative line already shown to maintain matrix engagement is the comparison that lacks the fusion. No mice and no patient specimens are required.

The primary inhibitor is adavosertib (AZD1775), the agent in ADVL1312. **CONFIRM** the compound used for the AACR experiments. If it was not adavosertib, name it and test adavosertib in parallel. WEE1 knockdown is the specificity control. WEE1 activity is read with the live-cell checkpoint reporter already in use or, if that reporter fails in collagen, with phospho-CDK1 Tyr15 on fixed spheroids. Proliferation uses the paired live-cell reporter, backed up by a fixed marker.

Each spheroid is the biological unit. Endpoints are fixed before treated and control images are compared: radial invasion, collagen-fiber orientation at the edge (the 2025 metric), spheroid growth, and the fraction of follower-zone cells positive for proliferation and WEE1 activity. The invasive front is the outer rim in contact with radially aligned collagen. The follower zone is the cell layer immediately inside that rim. Zones are defined by position, not by the marker being scored. Independent experiments, not technical wells, are the replicates.

## Preliminary data

The 2025 paper showed that fusion-positive tumors downregulate cell–matrix and TGFβ programs relative to fusion-negative tumors, that TGFβ increases matrix engagement in fusion-negative cells, and that PAX3-FOXO1 raises NOS1 and nitric oxide, which suppresses TGFβ and matrix interaction. Knockdown restores matrix engagement, and fusion status predicts sensitivity when that interaction is disrupted.

The 2026 abstract adds the invasion context. Followers have higher PAX3-FOXO1, higher proliferation, and higher WEE1 activity. Knockdown RNA-seq is negatively enriched for G1/S and DNA-replication sets. The abstract also reports an effect on S-phase and lower pCDK2 and γH2AX, and it reports that pharmacologic WEE1 inhibition slowed growth and invasion. ChIP-seq was listed as part of that plan. **CONFIRM** whether those chromatin data are interpretable. If they are not, Aim 1 uses the existing knockdown RNA-seq and published PAX3-FOXO1 chromatin maps.

## Aim 1. The follower compartment

Months 1–8. In Rh41 and the second fusion-positive line, at a time when collective invasion is underway, we will quantify PAX3-FOXO1, proliferation, WEE1 activity, and collagen alignment in the leader rim and the follower layer of the same spheroids. The same measurements follow PAX3-FOXO1 knockdown and are made in the fusion-negative comparison. The prediction is that a WEE1-high proliferative layer is present in fusion-positive controls, reduced after knockdown, and not organized the same way in fusion-negative spheroids, which instead engage matrix more strongly.

The knockdown RNA-seq is then set beside the images. G1/S and DNA-replication genes that fall after knockdown are checked for a follower-layer pattern. This distinguishes the two readings of the preliminary data. If high PAX3-FOXO1 marks proliferative followers, knockdown should shrink that layer. If the dominant effect is S-phase arrest, knockdown should increase the proliferative fraction. The same experiment can show either result, and we will report the one the images show.

If live reporters fail because of scatter, fixed staining of PAX3-FOXO1, a proliferation marker, and phospho-CDK1 is enough. The follower layer of interest is near the rim, not the spheroid core. We will not redefine the zones after seeing the WEE1 stain.

## Aim 2. When WEE1 inhibition matters

Months 6–20. Fusion-positive spheroids are treated across a concentration range of adavosertib that includes exposures already shown to inhibit WEE1 in published pediatric preclinical studies. On-target effect in our spheroids, read as loss of the WEE1-activity signal, is required before any concentration is called active. Endpoints are growth, radial invasion, and the size of the Aim 1 follower compartment. WEE1 knockdown must phenocopy the inhibitor. If the drug changes invasion and knockdown does not, the drug effect is off-target and will not be called a WEE1 result.

An on-target concentration, if one exists, is then repeated in parental fusion-positive cells, in PAX3-FOXO1-knockdown cells, and after matrix anchorage is shifted with the NOS1–nitric oxide–TGFβ manipulation already validated here. The prediction is that WEE1 inhibition matters most in parental spheroids that still have a proliferative follower layer. If changing anchorage alone removes that layer, WEE1 dependence and matrix independence are one state. If the layer and the drug effect persist after matrix engagement is restored, they are separable, and a matrix-targeted combination is not justified by these data.

No invasion effect at on-target exposures is a completed aim. It would agree with the negative rhabdomyosarcoma cohort of ADVL1312 and will be reported that way. Nonspecific toxicity is a separate pitfall: growth, invasion, and follower-marker loss must move together, and the knockdown must agree. If the drug is cytostatic in two-dimensional culture but inactive in collagen, the three-dimensional endpoints still decide the aim.
